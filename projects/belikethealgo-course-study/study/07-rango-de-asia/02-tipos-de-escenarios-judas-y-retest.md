## Two course scenarios — instructor explicit

Benjamin presents two common daily scenarios around the Asia accumulation range:

1. **Asian Break Retest**
2. **Judas**

He repeatedly says these are common/high-probability patterns, especially Judas. These frequency claims are not accepted as statistical facts until the weekly corpus is measured.

## Asian Break Retest

Approx. 74–216 s.

### Bullish schematic

```text
Asia accumulation
-> break above Asia high
-> later retest toward/into Asia boundary
-> during retest, take a local low / interact with a relevant POI below
-> continuation higher
```

### Bearish schematic

Mirror logic:

```text
Asia accumulation
-> break below Asia low
-> later retest toward/into Asia boundary
-> during retest, take a local high / interact with a relevant POI above
-> continuation lower
```

### Timing variants explicitly described

Benjamin gives two common timing arrangements:

- London makes the initial expansion and New York can later perform the retest/continuation.
- Frankfurt/period before London makes the initial break, then London open performs the retest and continuation.

A particularly repeated heuristic is:

```text
if Asia high/low is already broken before London open,
Asian Break Retest becomes a scenario to watch at London open.
```

This is a **scenario-prior / conditional hypothesis**, not a deterministic rule. One example explicitly breaks early and simply continues without giving the expected retest.

## Judas

Approx. 216–394 s.

Benjamin's preferred scenario is the Judas manipulation.

### Bullish Judas

```text
Asia accumulation
-> price breaches/manipulates below Asia low
-> market appears to be selling
-> reaction from liquidity/POI
-> directional move upward, often toward/through the opposite side
```

### Bearish Judas

Mirror logic:

```text
Asia accumulation
-> price breaches/manipulates above Asia high
-> market appears to be buying
-> reaction from liquidity/POI
-> directional move downward, often toward/through the opposite side
```

The conceptual diagrams visually confirm this opposite-side manipulation and reversal logic.

## Importance of location / POI

Benjamin repeatedly asks whether a relevant orderblock/POI lies just outside the Asia boundary. The range edge itself provides liquidity; the POI helps explain where a manipulation may terminate and react.

However, consistent with module 5, the orderblock is a **confluence**, not a universal mandatory gate. The key sequence is still liquidity + valid timing + actual reaction.

## Timing evidence

Many examples are centered on London open / London killzone. Benjamin distinguishes examples that manipulate around 03:00 New York from similar-looking moves that occur around Frankfurt/02:00 and says he is less interested when they occur outside his intended operating time.

Therefore the geometric pattern alone is insufficient:

```text
Judas geometry outside valid operating time != automatically valid trade
```

## Examples that do not behave perfectly — important negative evidence

This lesson contains several useful counterexamples:

- An early Asia break where price never performs the expected London retest and simply accelerates.
- News/FOMC-type candles that distort the normal pattern; Benjamin explicitly calls such days difficult/abnormal to trade.
- A Judas-style manipulation that reacts but fails to break the opposite Asia boundary and begins consolidating.
- Moves that occur one or two hours before the preferred London timing and therefore may leave no valid entry later.

These examples are critical: the scenario label does not guarantee target completion or even entry availability.

## Management statement

Around 1220–1280 s Benjamin uses a failed-follow-through Judas example to argue for taking partial profits because no strategy is always right. This is a management principle, but the lesson does **not** define a numerical partial-profit percentage or exact trigger. Record the concept; do not invent a partial rule.


Then classify observed post-Asia path without peeking beyond the time needed for each event:

### Candidate Judas bullish

```text

```

### Candidate Judas bearish

```text

```

### Candidate Break-Retest bullish

```text
price establishes break above AH before/around session
later returns toward AH / Asia range

```

### Candidate Break-Retest bearish

Mirror below AL.

The words `meaningful break`, `return toward`, `reaction` and `resume` deliberately remain parameterized; numerical thresholds must come from labelled examples and held-out validation.
